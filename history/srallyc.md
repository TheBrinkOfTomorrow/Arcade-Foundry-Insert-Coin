# Sega Rally Championship

Sega AM3 · 1995 · Sega Model 2A

Sega Rally Championship took the texture-mapped 3D of Daytona USA off the tarmac and onto dirt, gravel and mud. It was made by Sega's AM3 team, directed by Kenji Sasaki, who had worked on Namco's Ridge Racer, and produced by Tetsuya Mizuguchi. It is often called the first racing game in which a car handles differently depending on the surface under its wheels, so a slide on gravel feels nothing like one on tarmac.

You race a Toyota Celica GT-Four or a Lancia Delta HF Integrale through desert, forest and mountain stages in a short championship, and a strong run unlocks the extra Lakeside stage. Hidden in the arcade game is a third car, the Lancia Stratos. Up to four cabinets can be linked, and the rally co-driver's calls ("easy right, maybe!") became as well known as Takenobu Mitsuyoshi's music.

The Saturn version of 1995 was one of the best-loved arcade conversions of its time and sold over a million copies. The game started the Sega Rally series, with Sega Rally 2 on Model 3 in 1998, and Codemasters has named it as an influence on Colin McRae Rally. Retro Gamer once named it the greatest racing game ever made.
