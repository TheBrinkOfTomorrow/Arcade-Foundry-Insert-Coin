# Daytona USA

Sega AM2 · 1994 · Sega Model 2

Daytona USA was the first game on Sega's Model 2 board, and it showed off what the board was built for: texture-mapped 3D at around 60 frames a second, using technology Sega licensed from GE Aerospace. Where Virtua Racing's cars and tracks had been flat-shaded polygons, Daytona's had painted surfaces, crowds and scenery, and it looked a generation ahead of anything else in the arcade.

AM2, with Toshihiro Nagoshi directing, built it around American stock-car racing. You drive the Hornet on three tracks: the Three Seven Speedway oval for beginners, Dinosaur Canyon for advanced players and Sea-Side Street Galaxy for experts. Up to eight cabinets can be linked for one race, and big installations of four or eight seats became a fixture of arcades through the 1990s.

Its soundtrack is as famous as the game. Takenobu Mitsuyoshi wrote and sang the songs, and lines like "Daytona, let's go away!" made the game hard to miss on any arcade floor. The game is also full of secrets, from a Sonic the Hedgehog face carved into the canyon wall to a "rolling start" trick for a faster getaway.

It became one of Sega's most successful arcade games. The Saturn version followed in 1995 and Daytona USA Championship Circuit Edition in 1996, and the arcade sequel, Daytona USA 2, came out on Model 3 in 1998. This app has both the 1993 location-test version and the 1994 Revision A release.
